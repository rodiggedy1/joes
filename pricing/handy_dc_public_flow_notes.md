## Washington, DC Market Benchmarks

### Furniture Assembly

- **Source:** https://www.taskrabbit.com/locations/washington-dc/assemble-furniture
- **Published market observation:** Taskrabbit states that furniture assembly in Washington, DC costs around $48 per hour. The page includes provider examples with two-hour minimums.
- **Pricing implication:** A Good Joe baseline should be designed around a two-hour minimum, while simple one-item assembly can be expressed as a clear starting price rather than an open-ended hourly quote.

### TV Mounting

- **Sources:** https://www.taskrabbit.com/locations/washington-dc/wall-mount-tv and https://www.thumbtack.com/dc/washington/tv-wall-mount-install
- **Published market observations:** Taskrabbit states around $56 per hour for DC TV mounting. Thumbtack’s public cost guide gives national examples of $119 for a standard mount, $139 with in-wall wire concealment, $179–$249 for an above-fireplace mount with hidden wires, and notes that wall material and mount type materially affect cost.
- **Observed customer choices:** Desired timing; cable concealment; sound system; TV size above/below 60 inches; customer-owned mount/stand; mount type; nearby outlet; wall material; location (including above fireplace); and peripheral devices.
- **Pricing implication:** Good Joe should use a fixed one-TV drywall starting price, then show transparent add-ons for wall complexity, cable concealment, high/above-fireplace location, hardware, and devices.

### Handyman and Minor Home Repairs

- **Source:** https://www.angi.com/articles/what-are-common-handyman-prices/dc/washington
- **Published market observations:** Angi reports Washington, DC handyman projects average $506 and range from $218 to $795. Its local examples include $165–$545 for hanging shelves, $135–$385 for drywall patches, $110–$330 for faucet replacement, $110–$440 for ceiling-fan installation, $330–$440 for TV mounting, and $220–$1,750 per room for painting.
- **Pricing implication:** Good Joe should lead with a small-job/diagnostic visit or two-hour base, provide job-specific starting prices for simple requests, and require scope review for combined or trade-sensitive work.

### Lawn and Yard Care

- **Sources:** https://lawnguru.co/cities/washington-d-c-dc/lawn-mowing, https://www.taskrabbit.com/locations/washington-dc/yard-work-removal, and https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-lawn-mowing-cost/dc/washington
- **Published market observations:** LawnGuru lists DC mowing from $44, with a stated $44.97–$54.99 range and $49.98 average per cut; its recurring options are weekly, bi-weekly, and monthly. Angi reports DC mowing averages $171 and ranges from $59 to $314 depending on property size and scope.
- **Observed customer choices:** Property address/measurement; grass height; mowing frequency; one-time versus recurring; mowing, trimming, blowing, and edging. Taskrabbit’s public DC yardwork examples also point to weeding, hedge trimming, pruning, mulching, leaf cleanup, and waste-bag/haul requirements.
- **Pricing implication:** Good Joe should calculate mowing from property size and access, present a clear one-time base, offer recurring-frequency discounts, and scope cleanup, overgrowth, leaf hauling, garden work, and debris removal separately.

### Home Cleaning

- **Sources:** https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-it-cost-hire-house-cleaner/dc/washington and https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/house-cleaning-prices/
- **Published market observations:** Angi reports Washington, DC professional house cleaning averages $183 per visit with most customers spending $129–$237; its quoted local hourly average is about $50, within a $28–$80 range. Housecall Pro’s 2026 guide lists DC standard cleaning at $170–$270 for 2,000 square feet and $40–$65 per cleaner-hour. The same guide identifies recurring cleaning as commonly 10%–15% lower per visit than one-time work.
- **Observed customer choices from Handy:** Bedroom count, bathroom count, cleaning duration, appointment date, and 30-minute arrival time; Handy recommends three hours for a one-bed/one-bath home.
- **Pricing implication:** Good Joe can begin with a simple bedroom/bathroom package, add a cleaning type (standard/deep/move-in/move-out), then offer clearly priced interior-appliance, baseboard, laundry, and extra-room add-ons.

### Moving Help

- **Sources:** https://www.micsmoving.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-hire-movers-in-dc and https://www.handy.com/services/moving-help/washington-dc
- **Published market observations:** A current DC mover guide lists two movers plus a truck at $120–$185 per hour, often with a three-hour minimum. Handy’s booking form instead structures the request by one to three professionals, certificate-of-insurance need, moving-materials purchase, job description, and two to 10 hours in 30-minute steps.
- **Pricing implication:** Good Joe should distinguish **moving labor only** (one or two pros and no truck) from a full moving-company product. Its form should ask pro count, hours, floors/elevator, load/unload/both, heavy/special items, building certificate requirement, and customer-supplied truck.

### Plumbing and Electrical

- **Sources:** https://www.taskrabbit.com/locations/washington-dc/plumbing, https://www.taskrabbit.com/locations/washington-dc/electrical-work, and https://www.handy.com/services/electrical?svc_redirect_skip=true
- **Published market observations:** Taskrabbit states around $71 per hour for Washington, DC plumbing help and around $67 per hour for DC general handyman work. Handy’s electrical intake uses item counts for lights, dimmers, and ceiling fans; ladder height; and a two-to-10-hour duration selection, and it explicitly excludes new wiring where wires are not already accessible.
- **Pricing implication:** Good Joe should use transparent fixed starts for existing-access fixture/switch/fan work and a diagnostic/repair visit for plumbing. New circuits, panel work, emergency repairs, gas work, major leaks, inaccessible wiring, and any work requiring a licensed trade specialist need a separate, explicitly confirmed scope rather than an instant checkout price.

### Junk Removal

- **Sources:** https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost/dc/washington, https://www.thumbtack.com/dc/washington/junk-removal, and https://www.fechmovingcompany.com/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost-in-washington-dc-maryland-virginia-2026-pricing-guide
- **Published market observations:** Angi reports Washington, DC junk removal averages $248 with $140–$372 typical; it cites $85–$140 for roughly one-eighth truckload and $140–$220 for one-quarter truckload. The local FECH guide places DC small pickups at $100–$175, one-quarter truck at $200–$350, one-half truck at $350–$550, and full truck at $700–$1,000. Thumbtack’s DC intake asks timing, junk type, haul size, stairs/elevator, and items over five feet.
- **Pricing implication:** Good Joe should request junk category, truckload-volume band, photos, floor/elevator/parking access, heavy items, and disposal-sensitive items. It should label appliance, mattress, electronics, hazardous material, construction debris, and same-day handling rules before committing to a price.

### Pressure Washing

- **Sources:** https://lawnguru.co/cities/washington-d-c-dc/pressure-washing, https://www.thumbtack.com/dc/washington/pressure-washing, and https://www.taskrabbit.com/cost-guides/pressure-washing
- **Published market observations:** Thumbtack states that some Washington, DC pros start around $75 and cites about $0.10–$0.17 per square foot. Taskrabbit’s September 1, 2026 guide gives Washington, DC averages of $72 per hour and $263 per invoice; its common-project guide lists patios/walkways at $50–$75, decks $100–$200, driveways $180–$240, siding $300–$600, and roofs $450–$700.
- **Observed customer choices:** Desired timing; building-size band; purpose (routine cleaning, paint/stain prep, grime, oil/grease, mold/moss); area (driveway/garage, siding, deck/patio/porch, sidewalk, roof); home/business; building height; outdoor-water availability. LawnGuru’s DC flow additionally starts by asking what needs washing: driveway, siding, deck, roof, or multiple areas.
- **Pricing implication:** Good Joe should start with a single ground-level patio/walkway price, ask the surface plus approximate area/size, and quote fixed add-ons for stain treatment, house height, no water source, roof/soft-wash work, and additional surfaces. It must never represent roof or high-access work as a routine instant checkout without qualified-provider confirmation.
